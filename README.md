# Third-Party Cyber Risk Assessment

**GRC Portfolio Project | Intermediate Level**

> A practical Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) case study aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0.

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project demonstrates a structured Third-Party Cyber Risk Assessment process for a fictional mid-sized Italian e-commerce company.

The objective is to identify, assess and manage cybersecurity risks arising from third-party relationships, outsourced services, supplier access, cloud environments and external data processing.

The assessment combines risk-based supplier due diligence, security control evaluation, inherent and residual risk scoring, framework mapping and remediation planning.

## 2. Business Context and Scope

The assessment covers five fictional third-party suppliers operating across different business functions.

## 3. Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows a structured risk management workflow:

1. Third-party identification and classification.
2. Risk-based supplier due diligence.
3. Cybersecurity risk identification and scenario definition.
4. Inherent risk assessment.
5. Security control evaluation and evidence review.
6. Residual risk assessment.
7. Risk treatment planning and ownership assignment.
8. Periodic monitoring and reassessment.

## 4. Risk Assessment Model

A 5x5 risk matrix is used to evaluate likelihood and impact.

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

| Score | Risk Level |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Medium |
| 10–15 | High |
| 16–25 | Critical |

The methodology distinguishes between:

- **Inherent Risk:** Exposure before considering security controls.
- **Residual Risk:** Remaining exposure after considering implemented and evidenced controls.

Risk treatment decisions are based on residual risk, organisational risk appetite and assigned ownership.

## 6. Security Controls and Framework Mapping

The assessment uses two complementary cybersecurity frameworks.

### ISO/IEC 27001:2022

Indicative Annex A control references include:

- A.5.15 – Access control
- A.5.18 – Access rights
- A.5.19 – Information security in supplier relationships
- A.5.20 – Addressing information security within supplier agreements
- A.5.21 – Managing information security in the ICT supply chain
- A.5.22 – Monitoring, review and change management of supplier services
- A.5.23 – Information security for use of cloud services
- A.5.34 – Privacy and protection of PII

### NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

Relevant categories and outcomes include:

- GV.SC – Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management
- ID.RA – Risk Assessment
- ID.AM – Asset Management
- PR.AA – Identity Management, Authentication and Access Control
- PR.DS – Data Security
- DE.AE – Adverse Event Analysis
- RS.MA – Incident Management
- RC.RP – Incident Recovery Plan Execution

Mappings are indicative and based on control and outcome intent, not one-to-one equivalence.

## 7. Risk Treatment and Remediation

The project includes a structured treatment plan with six remediation actions addressing the identified risk scenarios.

Each action includes:
- Risk reference and remediation objective.
- Priority and target completion date.
- Assigned action owner.
- Required acceptance evidence.
- Status and dependencies.

Treatment strategies focus on privileged access management, backup resilience, API security, supplier incident response, HR SaaS security and third-party data governance.
